Calendar of the moment

Working conversions use explicit civil or arithmetic conventions; no claim of a completed independent accuracy audit. Design previews are intentionally not today's dates. Religious sunset/sunrise boundaries, authentic pictograms and disputed historical continuations require additional research.
Calculation conventions and limitations

Gregorian and Julian are proleptic. Browser ICU handles civil Islamic, Hebrew, Persian, Ethiopian, Coptic, Indian national, Chinese, Japanese and Thai calendar fields where supported. Browser civil dates are used for these rather than observational religious days. French Republican uses the historical leap pattern followed by a Romme-style continuation. Darian uses Gangale's telescopic epoch and original 500-year leap rule. Maya Long Count and Tzolkʼin use GMT 584283, equivalent to 13.0.0.0.0 on 21 December 2012. Tōnalpōhualli uses Caso's 1521 correlation. The Moon is a leading-term approximation, not an observatory-quality ephemeris. Maramataka uses the separately calculated 2026 Wellington sky snapshots, with no invented stellar month boundaries. Some calendars are rendered as designs only, with placeholder pictograms. Clock faces are optional modern local time overlays, not traditional timekeeping systems.
